Hisar privacy policy

Effective 28 October 2026. Applies to the hosted Hisar service at eu.tryhisar.com, its notification relay at push.tryhisar.com, and use of that service through the Hisar apps for macOS, iPhone and Android, the hisar command-line tool and the Hisar browser extension. If you use an independently operated server, ask its operator for its privacy notice; the hosting and retention commitments here concern our hosted service.

Summary

  • Hisar is an end-to-end encrypted password, SSH key and secrets manager for developers. Your passwords, notes, keys and other items are encrypted on your devices before they are sent to our server. We cannot read them.
  • To run the service, the server also stores readable information: your email address and name, devices, sharing relationships, organizations and teams, notification registrations, timestamps, sizes, and IP addresses in a security log kept for one year. The categories and purposes are described below.
  • There is no analytics, advertising, tracking or selling of data. Our pages and emails contain no trackers and the service sets no cookies.

Who runs Hisar

The hosted service is operated by Alper Berber ("we", "us"), based in the Netherlands. We are the controller of the personal data described here. Questions and requests: support@tryhisar.com. Product help is available at Contact and support.

What we cannot see

During normal use, your apps encrypt vault contents before sending them to the server, and do not send your master password or Secret Key. A copy of the database does not provide the keys needed to decrypt your vaults. These protections cover the information below; the section “Security, and its limits” explains the different risks of compromised devices, sharing and server-served web pages:

  • your master password and your Secret Key (signing in uses a protocol, OPAQUE, in which the server never receives the password);
  • the contents of your items: titles, usernames, passwords, websites, notes, custom fields, one-time-password secrets, passkeys and SSH private keys;
  • vault names, including those of organization vaults;
  • what a share link contains, and the key or passphrase that opens it;
  • what an approval request from your Mac to your smart device describes, and your answer.

Because we never have your master password or Secret Key, we cannot reset your password or recover your data. If you lose both your master password and every signed-in device and your Emergency Kit, your data is lost.

Kept only in memory

  • Rate limits. To slow down password guessing and spam, the server counts requests per IP address and per account in memory. These counters are never written to disk and are lost when the server restarts.
  • Approvals from your smart device. When your Mac asks your smart device to approve an SSH or command-line request, the server relays an encrypted request and an encrypted answer. It sees your account, which device asked, an ID, the sizes and the times, for at most a few minutes, in memory only.
  • Live updates. While your apps are open, the server keeps a connection to tell them that something changed, without content.

Logs and backups

  • Like most web services, our web server records each request (IP address, time, address requested, response status and browser or app version) in access logs, kept for 14 days. Opening a share link is recorded there the same way; the part of the link that holds the key is never sent to the server.
  • We create encrypted nightly database snapshots and maintain point-in-time recovery backups in AWS Frankfurt, Germany (eu-central-1), with backup copies in a separate locked AWS account in Stockholm, Sweden (eu-north-1). All backup copies are deleted within 30 days. Data deleted from the live service may remain in those backups until they expire.

Email

The server sends transactional emails, including account invitations, vault and organization invitations, notices that sharing needs to be completed, credential-change notices and account-deletion confirmations. Messages can contain the names and email addresses of the people involved, organization names and invitation links; they do not contain vault names or item contents. These messages contain no tracking images. Email is not end-to-end encrypted. Cloudflare Email Service processes recipients' addresses and message contents to deliver these emails.

If you email support, we receive your address, message and attachments and use them to answer your request or investigate a problem. Our email providers process correspondence to deliver and store it. We keep it only as needed to handle the request, security issues or legal obligations. Account deletion does not automatically erase support correspondence; you can ask us to delete it. Never send your master password, Secret Key, Emergency Kit or vault contents.

Notifications

When you enable notifications, your account server stores each phone's relay handle (iPhone) or Web Push endpoint and subscription keys (Android), and the notification types it wants: approval, security and sharing. Turning off all notification types in Hisar removes the phone's notification registration from the server once the change reaches it. This does not itself revoke Apple's push token or unregister your Android UnifiedPush distributor.

  • iPhone: your app registers its Apple push token with Hisar's relay at push.tryhisar.com. The account server receives a relay handle, not the Apple token. The relay processes the token, notification type and delivery time, and Apple sees the type and timing for that token. Notifications may include additional details encrypted to your phone, such as the sender of a sharing invitation; the relay and Apple cannot read those encrypted details. Push notifications do not contain vault contents or the contents of approval requests. Approval requests are fetched separately through your server, end-to-end encrypted.
  • Android: your account server sends encrypted Web Push messages directly to your chosen UnifiedPush provider, not through Hisar's iPhone relay or Firebase. The provider can see arrival times, message sizes and the notification type in the HTTP Topic header, but cannot decrypt the message body.

The relay and its network proxy also process connection information, including IP addresses. A relay handle is not a promise of anonymity.

Who else receives data

  • The people you share with see your name, your email address and your security code, and the vaults you share with them. Apart from the admins of the Hisar server (below), nobody can look up whether an email address has a Hisar account or list its users.
  • The admins of the Hisar server, from its admin dashboard, see each account's email address, name, role, whether it is suspended, sign-up date, last activity, and how many devices and vaults it has, and each device's name, platform and when it signed in, was last seen and was signed out. They never see vault names, items or keys, which stay end-to-end encrypted. They can suspend an account, which keeps it and its devices from reaching the server and stops its share links from opening until they reactivate it, sign its devices out, change its role, and delete it, as described under "Your choices and rights", and you get an email when they delete it.
  • The people in your organizations see your name, your email address, your role, your security code and the teams you are in. An organization's admins see the email addresses invited to it, and whoever you invite sees your name and email address with the invitation. Admins choose who is in each team, and so who gets its vaults. Being an admin gives no vault: an admin who joins a team gets its vaults like any member, and every member sees that. People outside an organization cannot see who is in it.
  • Amazon Web Services (AWS) hosts the service, database and backups in Frankfurt, Germany, with backup copies in Stockholm, Sweden. Both storage regions are in the EU.
  • Cloudflare provides the network proxy and DDoS protection for eu.tryhisar.com and push.tryhisar.com, and transactional email delivery through Cloudflare Email Service. At its TLS edge, Cloudflare can process IP addresses, request metadata, session tokens and other service data that is not end-to-end encrypted. Vault contents remain encrypted in those requests.
  • Apple delivers iPhone notifications through Apple Push Notification service, as described above. On Android, notifications go to the UnifiedPush provider you choose.
  • Authorities, only when the law requires it. What we could hand over is what this page lists; we cannot decrypt your vaults.

We do not sell or rent personal data, and we do not use it for advertising or profiling.

Cloudflare routes requests through its global edge network and may process them at data centers outside the EU. Our proxy is not restricted to EU locations.

For transfers covered by their certifications, AWS and Cloudflare rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK extension where applicable. Their data processing terms provide Standard Contractual Clauses as a fallback when the Framework does not cover a transfer, with the UK Addendum where required. See AWS's data protection information and Cloudflare's Data Processing Addendum. Apple's privacy policy describes Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers of European and UK personal data. The apps

  • The macOS, iPhone and Android apps keep an encrypted copy of your vaults on the device so they work offline. The Android app keeps that copy and your Secret Key out of Android's backups and out of transfers to a new device. Touch ID, Face ID and Android's fingerprint and face unlock are handled by the device's operating system; Hisar never receives your fingerprint or face data.
  • For AutoFill on the iPhone, Hisar gives iOS the website and username of each login and passkey, so iOS can suggest them, and iOS keeps that list on the iPhone. Android's AutoFill gets nothing from Hisar ahead of time. When you fill a form, Android tells Hisar the page's address or the app you are in, and the form's fields; Hisar reads the app's signing certificate from the device. Hisar stores none of it, unless you choose to always use a login in that app, which saves the app's name and certificate in the login, encrypted like the rest of it. After you sign in somewhere, Android can offer to save what you typed to Hisar, and Hisar stores it only if you save it in its editor. On Android 14 and later, when a website in your browser or an app asks Android for a passkey or password, Android gives Hisar the website's address or the app's name and signing certificate, the site the request is for, the passkeys that site accepts and, when you save a passkey, your user name there; Hisar stores none of it unless you save a new passkey, and before it lets an app use a website's passkeys it reads that website's /.well-known/assetlinks.json from your device.
  • A password or other secret you copy is cleared from the clipboard after 90 seconds on the iPhone, where it also stays off your other devices. On Android it is cleared about 2 minutes after the copy and marked sensitive, so the system and the keyboards that respect the mark hide it. On Android, screenshots, screen recordings and the app switcher show nothing of Hisar unless you turn on Allow screenshots in Settings → Security.
  • The apps contact the Hisar server you sign in to, and ask the website of a login for its icon (Settings → Appearance → Show website icons turns this off on the Mac, the iPhone and Android). To fill a login into an Android app, Hisar may also ask a website in your vaults whose name is part of the app's name whether that app may use its logins, by reading the website's /.well-known/assetlinks.json; the website sees that request come from your device's IP address. The macOS app checks the Hisar server for updates while you are signed in.
  • The Hisar browser extension talks only to the Hisar app on the same computer, through the browser's native messaging, and sends nothing to the internet. It reads the address of the page you are on and the login forms on it, to offer matching logins and to offer saving a login after you sign in. Page contents are not stored or sent anywhere except to the Hisar app on your computer. It stores a random installation ID, the pairing token for your Hisar app, its settings, and the sites you chose "Never for this site" for, in the browser's local extension storage. Logins are filled only on the site they were saved for.

Security, and its limits

Traffic is encrypted with TLS. Items are encrypted with keys derived from your master password and your 128-bit Secret Key, so a copy of our database is not enough to guess your password offline. We work to keep the service secure, but please know the limits of any server-based design:

  • The server sees the metadata listed above: who you share with, which organizations and teams you are in, how many items you have, how big they are and when they change.
  • Cloudflare terminates TLS at its edge, so it can see readable service traffic, including session tokens, in transit. That does not decrypt end-to-end encrypted vault contents. Like the server, however, the proxy is part of the trust boundary for web pages it serves.
  • A compromised server could try to hand you someone else's public key when you share a vault. Comparing security codes with the other person once (Settings → People) detects that.
  • Verify the intended recipient when first sharing a vault. For organization vaults, your app relies on the server's team membership records when delivering vault keys; a compromised server could misrepresent that membership.
  • The Android app trusts every certificate authority installed on the device, including ones you install yourself, so a certificate authority you install can see Hisar's TLS traffic, while the contents of your vaults stay end-to-end encrypted.
  • The web pages for opening share links and signing in to the admin dashboard are served through our infrastructure. A compromised server or proxy could change those pages to capture a share-link key, opened contents or credentials entered there. People who use the hisar command-line tool can open share links without the page.

If we learn of a breach that affects your personal data, we will tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

Legal bases

Under the EU GDPR and, where applicable, UK GDPR we rely on:

  • Contract (Art. 6(1)(b)): account, device, vault, sharing, organization, preferences, enabled notifications and share-link data, and emails about your account, to provide the service you signed up for.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)): security logs, rate limits and access logs to protect the service; support correspondence to answer requests and investigate problems; and invitation emails to people who do not yet have an account, sent because a user asked us to.

Your choices and rights

  • You can see and change your vaults, devices, sharing and organizations in the apps at any time, and sign out any device.
  • You can delete your account in the Hisar apps (Settings → Account → Delete account) or with hisar account delete, after reviewing the consequences and confirming your master password and email address. You can also email support@tryhisar.com from the account's address for help, and a server admin can delete it from the admin dashboard. Deletion removes your devices, sessions, notification registrations, share links, affected invitations and vault access. Vaults for which you are the only owner, including their items, are deleted and people you shared them with lose access. Vaults with another owner remain. Your organization and team memberships are removed; remaining organizations can appoint a successor admin, and organizations left with no members are deleted. Retained shared records may still contain your account ID. We email you a confirmation. Security logs and backups follow their retention schedules above; copies other people already downloaded cannot be erased. Uninstalling an app does not delete the account on the server.
  • Under applicable EU or UK data protection law, you have rights to access your personal data, to have it corrected (rectification) or erased, to restrict its processing, to receive it in a portable format, and to object to processing based on legitimate interests. Write to support@tryhisar.com. We respond within one month; if a lawful extension is needed for a complex request, we explain it within that month. We may verify your identity, but will not ask for your master password or Secret Key.
  • You may also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority. We are based in the Netherlands, so the lead authority is the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl); you can also complain to the authority in the EU country where you live or work.
  • In the United Kingdom, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), the UK supervisory authority.
